OSHA Requirement (29 CFR 1926.404): All electrical installations and equipment must be approved, properly installed, and maintained. GFCI protection required for temporary power. Regular inspections prevent electrical hazards.

1. GFCI Protection & Temporary Power

All 120V temporary power receptacles protected by GFCI
GFCIs tested monthly (test button pushed, trips properly)
Temporary wiring properly supported and protected from damage
Power distribution boxes/spider boxes in good condition

2. Extension Cords & Power Tools

Extension cords rated for hard/extra-hard usage (no household cords)
No damaged, cut, or spliced extension cords in use
Extension cords have grounding pin (3-prong plugs intact)
Power tools double-insulated or properly grounded
No cords run through doorways, walls, or across walkways (trip hazard)

3. Electrical Panels & Installations

Electrical panels accessible (min. 3 ft clearance, not blocked)
Panel covers/doors in place, no exposed energized parts
Circuit breakers/fuses properly labeled
No unauthorized modifications or bypassed safety devices

4. Lock-Out/Tag-Out (LOTO) Compliance

LOTO procedures established for equipment maintenance
Locks, tags, and LOTO devices available and in use
Workers trained on LOTO procedures

5. Overhead Power Lines & Clearances

Overhead power lines identified and marked
Minimum clearance maintained (10 ft for up to 50kV lines)
Equipment operators aware of power line locations
⚠ ELECTRICAL SHOCK CAN BE FATAL: Never work on energized circuits. De-energize, lock-out, tag-out, and test before work. Contact licensed electrician for repairs.
